Getting NUR — the exact steps, including the awkward ones

Arc is a young chain. Buying anything on it takes one step that other chains don't: getting USDC onto Arc. This page walks through it honestly, including the parts that are inconvenient.

Read this first. The NUR/USDC pool holds $2.55. A $1 buy moves the price roughly 40%. A $5 buy moves it about 200%. If you are thinking of this as an investment, stop — it is not one. If you are curious what a real pool on a new chain behaves like, $1 is plenty.

What you need

  • An EVM wallet (MetaMask, Rabby, anything that speaks WalletConnect)
  • USDC — about $2 is plenty
  • A few cents of that USDC for gas. On Arc, gas is USDC

The steps

  1. Add Arc to your wallet Chain ID 5042, RPC https://rpc.mainnet.arc.io, symbol USDC, explorer https://explorer.arc.io. Your wallet may warn that the gas symbol is not ETH — that is expected, Arc uses USDC.
  2. Get USDC onto Arc — this is the awkward step Arc has no faucet, so the USDC must come from somewhere real. Two routes that work:

    Route A — an exchange that lists Arc. Kraken and Upbit were in Arc's launch cohort. Withdraw USDC directly to your Arc address. This is the simplest route by a wide margin.

    Route B — bridge with Circle CCTP. Hold USDC on Base, Ethereum, Arbitrum or another supported chain and bridge it across. Use only interfaces linked from Circle's own CCTP documentation — fake "Arc bridge" sites exist and they take your money. Send a small test amount first.

    Sending USDC to an Arc address on the wrong network means permanent loss. Arc is not Base, not Ethereum, not Arbitrum.
  3. Swap USDC for NUR The pair is live on Uniswap v2 on Arc.
    Open Uniswap ↗ See the chart ↗

    Set a slippage tolerance of 5–10%. On a pool this thin, the default will often fail — that is the pool, not a bug.

  4. Add NUR to your wallet so it is visible Use the Noor Pay page — "Add NUR to wallet" does it in one click, or add the contract address manually with 18 decimals.

If you would rather not trade at all

That is a completely reasonable answer, and probably the correct one. NUR has no utility yet. A payment tool exists, a market exists, and the code is verified — but none of that is a reason for you to spend money.
