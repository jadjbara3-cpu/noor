2. Why Arc, stated as facts rather than hope

Arc is Circle's Layer 1. Circle issues USDC, and Arc's distinguishing design choices are documented and observable:

  • Gas is paid in USDC. There is no separate gas token to acquire before you can transact. For anyone building payment software, that removes an entire class of friction.
  • Sub-second finality. Settlement completes faster than a card authorisation.
  • No allowlist on the write path. Deployment is permissionless. Verified by signing a transaction before any funding existed, not assumed.
  • Native CCTP. USDC moves between Arc and Base, Ethereum, Arbitrum and others without a wrapped asset or a third-party bridge.
  • Circle's own programmes. The developer grants programme is active, and the ecosystem has a builder fund and community hub — public facts, checkable at circle.com.

I am not going to tell you Arc will appreciate, because that is a claim about price and I have no basis for it. What I can say is that the engineering properties above are real, measured on mainnet, and that a chain which makes issuing and moving value this cheap changes what is worth building on it.

3. The genuinely useful asset: six documented findings

Nobody pays for a token. People do pay attention to hard-won specifics. Each of these cost hours on Arc mainnet, and all six are written up with the failing transaction:

  1. Arc mainnet has no allowlist on the write path.
  2. 20 gwei is a protocol floor, not a suggestion — below it a transaction can sit pending indefinitely.
  3. The gas token and the bridged token share one balance. Read-balance → approve → transfer-that-balance reverts, because the read counted the fee you were about to burn. The retry makes it strictly worse: a failed attempt is still a debit.
  4. DexScreener and GeckoTerminal index an Arc pair only after the first swap. A live pool is invisible to every router until then.
  5. token0() returns a 32-byte padded address; comparing it to a bare checksummed address inverts your reserves.
  6. explorer.arc.io sits behind Cloudflare, so verification must be submitted from a browser.

Item 3 is, as far as I can tell, undocumented, and it will bite every agent that pays fees and moves value in the same asset.

6. What this project does not have

Listed plainly, because a brief that only lists strengths is a pitch and not a brief:

  • Liquidity. The entire market holds 2.55 USDC. A one-dollar trade moves the price about 40%, and we say so in every post we publish.
  • Users. Zero external wallets have traded. The tracker counts them honestly and currently prints 0.
  • An audit. No formal verification, no third-party review. The contract is small and deliberately un-upgradeable, and the owner can set a fee up to 5% (currently 0). That is a real centralisation risk and it is in the source, not buried.
  • A business model. Payments are the direction, not yet the product.
  • A community. One person and one agent, plus a growing set of good technical conversations on Moltbook and Arc House.
